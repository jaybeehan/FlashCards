## Social Studies 9 | Issues for Canadians: Governance and Rights
What is a constitutional monarchy? :: A system where a monarch is head of state but power is limited by the constitution.
What is responsible government? :: The government must keep the confidence of the elected House.
What is the rule of law? :: Everyone, including government, must obey the law.
What is federalism? :: Power is divided between federal and provincial governments.
What is Parliament made of? :: The Crown, the Senate and the House of Commons.
What is the Speaker of the House? :: The MP who presides over debates.
What is a caucus? :: The members of a political party in the legislature.
What is party discipline? :: Members voting with their party.
What is a whip? :: An MP who ensures party members vote together.
What is a confidence vote? :: A vote deciding whether the government keeps power.
What is prorogation? :: Ending a session of Parliament without dissolving it.
What is dissolution? :: Ending Parliament so an election can be held.
What is a writ of election? :: An order calling an election.
What is an election campaign? :: The period when candidates seek votes.
What is a platform? :: A party's plan and promises.
What is a backbencher? :: An MP who is not in Cabinet or opposition leadership.
What is a private member's bill? :: A bill introduced by an MP who is not a minister.
What are the steps for a bill to become law? :: First reading, second reading, committee, third reading, Senate, Royal Assent.
What is committee stage? :: Detailed review of a bill by a small group of MPs.
What is a Speech from the Throne? :: A speech outlining the government's agenda.
What is a budget? :: A government plan for revenue and spending.
What is the Auditor General? :: An officer who audits government spending.
What is the Chief Electoral Officer? :: The official overseeing federal elections.
What is Elections Canada? :: The agency running federal elections.
Who can vote in Canada? :: Canadian citizens aged 18 or older.
What is a secret ballot? :: A vote made in private.
What is a voters list? :: A list of eligible voters.
What is gerrymandering? :: Drawing riding boundaries to favour a party.
What is an independent candidate? :: A candidate not belonging to a party.
What is a coalition government? :: A government formed by two or more parties.
What is the Alberta Legislature? :: The provincial lawmaking body in Edmonton.
How many provinces and territories does Canada have? :: Ten provinces and three territories.
What is a territory's difference from a province? :: Territories have powers delegated by the federal government.
What is Indigenous self-government? :: Indigenous peoples governing their own affairs.
What is a band council? :: The governing body of a First Nation under the Indian Act.
What is the Assembly of First Nations? :: A national organization representing First Nations.
What is the Metis Nation of Alberta? :: A group representing Metis in Alberta.
What is the Inuit Tapiriit Kanatami? :: A national Inuit organization.
What is a treaty? :: An agreement between nations.
What is the Royal Proclamation of 1763? :: It recognized Indigenous land rights.
What are the Numbered Treaties? :: Eleven treaties from 1871 to 1921 covering much of Canada.
What was the Indian Act's pass system? :: A system requiring permission to leave reserves.
What was the potlatch ban? :: A law banning Indigenous ceremonies on the West Coast.
What is the Constitution's Section 35? :: It recognizes Aboriginal and treaty rights.
What is the Charter of Rights and Freedoms? :: The part of the Constitution protecting fundamental rights.
What does fundamental freedoms include? :: Conscience, religion, thought, expression, assembly and association.
What do legal rights include? :: Life, liberty, security, protection from unreasonable search and arbitrary detention.
What are equality rights? :: Equal treatment and benefit of the law without discrimination.
What are mobility rights? :: The right to live and work anywhere in Canada.
What are language rights? :: Rights to use English or French in federal institutions.
What does Section 1 do? :: It allows reasonable limits on rights in a free and democratic society.
What does Section 33 do? :: It lets governments override some Charter rights temporarily.
What is the Canadian Bill of Rights? :: A 1960 federal law protecting rights, before the Charter.
What is the Canadian Human Rights Commission? :: An agency promoting equality and investigating discrimination.
What is Alberta Human Rights Commission? :: The provincial body handling discrimination complaints.
What is discrimination? :: Unfair treatment based on characteristics such as race or religion.
What is a hate crime? :: A crime motivated by prejudice against a group.
What is freedom of expression's limit? :: It excludes hate speech and incitement to violence.
What is the Oakes test? :: A test used to decide if a limit on a right is justified.
What is the Persons Case? :: A 1929 decision that women are legal persons in Canada.
What is the Famous Five? :: Five Alberta women who won the Persons Case.
What is the Alberta Individual's Rights Protection Act? :: A past provincial human rights law.
What was the Komagata Maru? :: A 1914 ship of South Asian passengers refused entry.
What was the Chinese Head Tax? :: A fee for Chinese immigrants entering Canada.
What was the internment of Japanese Canadians? :: Forced relocation during WWII.
What was Ukrainian internment? :: Internment of Ukrainians during WWI.
What is an apology for past wrongs? :: An official acknowledgement and redress, like the 2008 residential school apology.
What is the Truth and Reconciliation Commission's goal? :: To tell the truth about residential schools and promote reconciliation.
What are the Calls to Action? :: 94 recommendations from the TRC.
What is UNDRIP? :: The UN Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples.
What is Orange Shirt Day? :: September 30, honouring residential school survivors.
What is the National Day for Truth and Reconciliation? :: September 30.
What is the role of the Supreme Court? :: Final court of appeal and interprets the Charter.
What is a precedent? :: A past ruling guiding future decisions.
What is a federal riding? :: A voting district for federal elections.
What is the Senate's role? :: Reviews and revises legislation.
How are senators chosen? :: Appointed by the Governor General on advice of the Prime Minister.
What is the Governor General's role? :: The Crown's representative; gives Royal Assent.
What is the Lieutenant Governor's role? :: The Crown's representative in a province.

## Social Studies 9 | Issues for Canadians: Economic Systems
What is an economy? :: The system of making, selling and buying goods and services.
What is a good? :: A physical product.
What is a service? :: Work done for others.
What is a need? :: Something required to survive.
What is a want? :: Something desired but not essential.
What is scarcity? :: Limited resources compared to wants.
What is opportunity cost? :: The value of the next best alternative.
What is a trade-off? :: Giving up one thing to get another.
What is a market? :: A place or system where buyers and sellers trade.
What is supply? :: The amount sellers offer.
What is demand? :: The amount buyers want.
What does a supply curve show? :: Quantity supplied at each price.
What does a demand curve show? :: Quantity demanded at each price.
What is equilibrium? :: The price where supply equals demand.
What happens to price if demand increases? :: Price rises.
What happens to price if supply increases? :: Price falls.
What is a shortage? :: Demand exceeds supply.
What is a surplus? :: Supply exceeds demand.
What is competition? :: Businesses vying for customers.
What is a monopoly? :: One seller controls a market.
What is an oligopoly? :: A few sellers dominate a market.
What is a traditional economy? :: Based on customs and subsistence.
What is a command economy? :: Government makes economic decisions.
What is a market economy? :: Individuals and firms decide through markets.
What is a mixed economy? :: A blend of market and government direction.
What is capitalism? :: An economic system with private ownership for profit.
What is socialism in economics? :: Public ownership and planning for equality.
What is communism in economics? :: Collective ownership of resources.
What is free enterprise? :: Businesses operate with minimal government control.
What is a Crown corporation? :: A government-owned company, like Canada Post.
What is a subsidy? :: Government money supporting an industry.
What is a tax? :: Money collected by government.
What is income tax? :: Tax on earnings.
What is sales tax? :: Tax on purchases.
What is the GST? :: Goods and Services Tax, 5% federally.
What is property tax? :: Tax on land and buildings, paid to municipalities.
What are royalties? :: Payments for natural resources extracted.
What is a deficit? :: Spending exceeds revenue.
What is a surplus in a budget? :: Revenue exceeds spending.
What is debt? :: Money owed.
What is inflation? :: A general rise in prices.
What is deflation? :: A general fall in prices.
What is unemployment? :: People without jobs who want work.
What is GDP? :: The total value of goods and services produced in a country.
What is per capita? :: Per person.
What is a recession? :: A period of economic decline.
What is a depression? :: A severe, long recession.
What is an economic boom? :: A period of rapid growth.
What is the business cycle? :: Alternating periods of expansion and contraction.
What is the Bank of Canada? :: Canada's central bank.
What is the prime rate? :: The interest rate banks offer their best customers.
What is a stock exchange? :: A market for buying and selling shares.
What is a share? :: A unit of ownership in a company.
What is a dividend? :: A payment from profits to shareholders.
What is entrepreneurship? :: Starting and running a business.
What is profit? :: Revenue minus costs.
What are the four factors of production? :: Land, labour, capital and entrepreneurship.
What is human capital? :: Skills and knowledge of workers.
What is a labour union? :: A group of workers bargaining together.
What is collective bargaining? :: Negotiations between unions and employers.
What is a strike? :: Workers stopping work to press demands.
What is a minimum wage? :: The lowest legal hourly pay.
What is the Alberta Advantage? :: A phrase for low taxes and resource wealth.
What is the Heritage Savings Trust Fund? :: An Alberta fund saving resource revenue.
What is diversification? :: Developing many industries.
What are Alberta's key industries? :: Energy, agriculture, forestry, tourism and technology.
What is the primary sector? :: Extracting raw materials.
What is the secondary sector? :: Manufacturing.
What is the tertiary sector? :: Services.
What is the quaternary sector? :: Knowledge-based services and research.
What is a trade agreement? :: A deal setting rules for trade.
What is a tariff? :: A tax on imports.
What is an export? :: A good sold to another country.
What is an import? :: A good bought from another country.
What is the trade balance? :: Exports minus imports.
What is consumer protection? :: Laws that protect buyers from unfair practices.
What is sustainable development? :: Growth that protects the future.
What is fair trade? :: Trade ensuring fair pay and conditions for producers.

## Social Studies 30-2 | Understandings of Ideology
What is an ideology? :: A set of ideas guiding political and economic beliefs.
What is liberalism? :: An ideology valuing individual rights and freedoms.
What is conservatism? :: An ideology valuing tradition and stability.
What is socialism? :: An ideology valuing equality and public ownership.
What is communism? :: An ideology seeking a classless society.
What is fascism? :: An authoritarian nationalist ideology.
What is democracy? :: Government by the people.
What is direct democracy? :: Citizens vote directly on laws.
What is representative democracy? :: Citizens elect representatives.
What is a dictatorship? :: Rule by one person.
What is a monarchy? :: Rule by a king or queen.
What is a republic? :: A state where power rests with citizens and elected officials.
What is a constitution? :: The basic rules of a state.
What is the Magna Carta? :: A 1215 charter limiting the English king.
What was the French Revolution? :: The 1789 overthrow of the monarchy.
What was the Declaration of the Rights of Man? :: A 1789 French statement of equal rights.
What was the American Revolution? :: The war for US independence.
What was the Industrial Revolution? :: The shift to factory production.
What was the Russian Revolution? :: The 1917 communist revolution.
What was the Great Depression? :: A 1930s economic crisis.
What was the Cold War? :: The US-Soviet rivalry.
What was the Iron Curtain? :: The Cold War divide in Europe.
What was the Berlin Wall? :: A barrier dividing Berlin from 1961 to 1989.
What ended the Cold War? :: The collapse of the Soviet Union in 1991.
What was perestroika? :: Gorbachev's restructuring.
What was glasnost? :: Gorbachev's openness policy.
What is a welfare state? :: A state providing social supports.
What is capitalism? :: Private ownership for profit.
What is a market economy? :: Decisions made by buyers and sellers.
What is a planned economy? :: Government makes economic decisions.
What is a mixed economy? :: Market with government involvement.
What is collectivism? :: Valuing group over individual.
What is individualism? :: Valuing the individual over the group.
What are individual rights? :: Rights held by each person.
What are collective rights? :: Rights held by groups.
What is human rights legislation? :: Laws against discrimination.
What was the Persons Case? :: The 1929 ruling that women are persons.
What was the women's suffrage movement? :: The campaign for women's right to vote.
When did Alberta women win the vote? :: 1916.
Who was Nellie McClung? :: A suffragist and one of the Famous Five.
What is civil disobedience? :: Peaceful lawbreaking to protest.
Who was Gandhi? :: Leader of India's non-violent independence movement.
Who was Martin Luther King Jr.? :: Leader of the US civil rights movement.
What was the civil rights movement? :: The struggle for equality for Black Americans.
What was apartheid? :: South Africa's racial segregation system.
What is censorship? :: Suppressing information.
What is a free press? :: Media free from government control.
What is propaganda? :: Biased information to sway opinion.
What is a secret police? :: An agency monitoring and repressing dissent.
What is the Gulag? :: Soviet forced labour camps.
What is a show trial? :: A staged trial for political purposes.
What was the Hitler Youth? :: A Nazi youth organization.
What was the Holocaust? :: The murder of six million Jews and others by Nazis.
What was Nazi Germany's economy? :: A state-directed economy for war.
What was Mussolini's role? :: Founder of Italian fascism.
What was Stalin's role? :: Soviet dictator from the 1920s to 1953.
What was Mao Zedong's role? :: Founder of communist China.
What is a political party? :: A group organized to win elections.
What is voter turnout? :: The percent of eligible voters who vote.
What is civic responsibility? :: Participating in and caring for your community.
What is lobbying? :: Trying to influence government decisions.
What is a protest? :: A public expression of objection.
What is a referendum? :: A direct vote on an issue.
What is a plebiscite? :: A vote on an issue that may not bind government.
What is media's role in politics? :: Informing citizens and holding power accountable.
What is social media's effect on politics? :: It mobilizes people but can spread misinformation.
What is a global ideology? :: An ideology spread across many countries, like liberal democracy.
What are the characteristics of a democratic society? :: Free elections, rule of law and protected rights.
