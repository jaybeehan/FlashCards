## Social Studies 9 | Issues for Canadians: Governance and Rights
What year did Canada become a country? :: 1867 (Confederation).
Who is Canada's current head of state? :: The monarch (King Charles III), represented by the Governor General.
What does the Governor General do? :: Gives Royal Assent to bills, opens Parliament and performs ceremonial duties.
What is Royal Assent? :: The formal approval that turns a bill into law.
What is the Cabinet? :: Ministers chosen by the Prime Minister to lead government departments.
What is the Official Opposition? :: The party with the second most seats in the House of Commons.
What is question period? :: A daily time when MPs question the government.
What is an MP? :: A Member of Parliament, elected to represent a riding.
What is an MLA? :: A Member of the Legislative Assembly, elected provincially (in Alberta).
What is a premier? :: The head of a provincial government.
What is a mayor's role? :: Leader of a municipal government.
What does a municipal government handle? :: Local services such as roads, water, garbage and parks.
What do provinces control? :: Education, health care, natural resources and property laws.
What is the federal government responsible for? :: Defence, foreign affairs, currency, criminal law and immigration.
What is the Constitution Act, 1982? :: It patriated the Constitution and included the Charter.
What is patriation? :: Bringing the Constitution home from the UK to Canada.
What is an amendment formula? :: The rules for changing the Constitution.
What is a by-election? :: An election held to fill a vacant seat.
What is first-past-the-post? :: The candidate with the most votes in a riding wins.
What is proportional representation? :: Seats are given according to each party's share of votes.
What is a voter's duty? :: To be informed and to vote.
What is a plebiscite? :: A vote on an issue that does not bind the government.
What is lobbying? :: Trying to influence government decisions.
What is civil disobedience? :: Peacefully breaking a law to protest it.
What is the Canadian Human Rights Act? :: A federal law prohibiting discrimination.
What is the Alberta Bill of Rights? :: A provincial law protecting rights and freedoms.
What is the Metis Settlements Act? :: An Alberta law giving Metis settlements self-government.
Who are the Metis? :: A distinct Indigenous people with mixed First Nations and European ancestry.
What are Inuit? :: Indigenous peoples of the Arctic, such as in Nunavut.
What is Section 35 of the Constitution? :: It recognizes and affirms existing Aboriginal and treaty rights.
What is a land claim? :: A legal claim by Indigenous groups to land or rights.
What is the Royal Proclamation of 1763? :: A document recognizing Indigenous rights to land.
What is a residential school survivor? :: A person who attended a residential school.
What is the Sixties Scoop? :: The removal of Indigenous children from families into non-Indigenous homes.
What is multiculturalism policy? :: Canada's approach valuing and protecting cultural diversity.
What is the Quebec Act? :: A 1774 law that protected French civil law and Catholic religion.
What is bilingualism? :: Official use of two languages, English and French, in federal services.
What is the Supreme Court composed of? :: Nine judges appointed by the federal government.
What is judicial independence? :: Judges are free from political pressure.
What is an Order in Council? :: A decision made by Cabinet that has the force of law.
What is the "reasonable limits" clause? :: Section 1 of the Charter allows limits that are justified in a free and democratic society.
What does "equality rights" protect? :: Equal treatment under the law without discrimination.
What is mobility rights? :: The right to live and work anywhere in Canada.
What is minority language education rights? :: The right to education in French or English where numbers warrant.
What is a class action? :: A lawsuit brought by a group with a common claim.
What is a tribunal? :: A body that resolves disputes outside regular courts.
What is a recall? :: A way for voters to remove an elected official.
What is the purpose of the Ombudsman? :: To investigate complaints against government.
What is the right to peaceful assembly? :: The right to gather in groups for a lawful purpose.

## Social Studies 9 | Issues for Canadians: Economic Systems
What is a factor of production? :: A resource used to produce goods: land, labour, capital, entrepreneurship.
What is capital in economics? :: Tools, machines and money used to produce goods.
What is a consumer? :: A person who buys goods and services.
What is a producer? :: A person or business that makes goods or provides services.
What is a price ceiling? :: A legal maximum price.
What is a price floor? :: A legal minimum price, like a minimum wage.
What is the law of demand? :: As price rises, quantity demanded falls.
What is the law of supply? :: As price rises, quantity supplied rises.
What is equilibrium price? :: The price at which supply equals demand.
What is a shortage? :: Demand exceeds supply.
What is a surplus? :: Supply exceeds demand.
What is an economic indicator? :: A statistic that shows economic health, like unemployment rate.
What is the unemployment rate? :: The percent of the labour force looking for work.
What is a labour force? :: People working or actively looking for work.
What is the consumer price index (CPI)? :: A measure of changes in prices of a basket of goods.
What is a boom and bust cycle? :: Rapid growth followed by sharp decline, common with resource economies.
What is the Alberta Heritage Savings Trust Fund? :: A fund created in 1976 to save part of resource revenue.
What are royalties? :: Payments to the government for resource extraction.
What is diversification? :: Spreading an economy across many industries.
What is the primary sector? :: Industries that extract raw materials (farming, mining, oil).
What is the secondary sector? :: Industries that manufacture goods.
What is the tertiary sector? :: Service industries.
What is a cooperative? :: A business owned and run by its members.
What is a franchise? :: A business using a parent company's name and methods.
What is a sole proprietorship? :: A business owned by one person.
What is a corporation? :: A business that is a separate legal entity owned by shareholders.
What is a shareholder? :: An owner of shares in a company.
What is a stock market? :: A place where shares of companies are bought and sold.
What is interest? :: A fee paid for borrowing money or earned on savings.
What is credit? :: Borrowing money to be repaid later.
What is a deficit? :: When a government spends more than it takes in.
What is a national debt? :: The total amount a government owes.
What does progressive taxation mean? :: Higher income is taxed at higher rates.
What is the GST? :: A 5% federal tax on most goods and services.
What are social programs? :: Government services such as health care and education.
What is Employment Insurance? :: A federal program that gives temporary income to unemployed workers.
What is the Canada Pension Plan? :: A public pension that workers pay into for retirement.
What is the Wheat Board's old role? :: It marketed Prairie wheat and barley until 2012.
What is a tariff? :: A tax on imported goods.
What is a trade deficit? :: Imports exceed exports.
What is a trade surplus? :: Exports exceed imports.
What is the Canadian dollar called? :: The loonie (the $1 coin).
What is the exchange rate? :: The price of one currency in another.
What is globalization's effect on prices? :: It often lowers prices through competition.
What is sustainable development? :: Growth that meets present needs without harming future generations.
What is ethical consumerism? :: Buying goods based on fair and sustainable practices.

## Social Studies 10-1 | Perspectives on Globalization
What are the effects of globalization on economies? :: More trade and investment, but possible job losses in some sectors.
What is a transnational corporation? :: A company that operates in several countries.
What is the International Monetary Fund? :: An organization that provides loans and monitors global finances.
What is the World Bank? :: An organization that lends to developing countries for projects.
What is the G7? :: A group of seven major advanced economies, including Canada.
What is the G20? :: A forum of 20 major economies.
What is NAFTA? :: The North American Free Trade Agreement (replaced by CUSMA).
What is CUSMA? :: The Canada-United States-Mexico Agreement.
What is the European Union? :: A political and economic union of European countries.
What is regionalization? :: Countries forming closer economic and political groups.
What is cultural imperialism? :: The dominance of one culture's media and values over others.
What is cultural hybridization? :: The blending of cultural elements into new forms.
What is the Canadian content (CanCon) rule? :: Rules requiring a share of Canadian music and programming on air.
What is a Quebec language law's aim? :: To protect French in the face of English-language globalization.
What is a lingua franca? :: A common language used between people of different languages.
What is Indigenous language revitalization? :: Efforts to preserve and teach endangered languages.
What is a sweatshop? :: A workplace with long hours, low pay and poor conditions.
What is the Fair Trade label? :: A label showing products meet fair pay and labour standards.
What is microfinance? :: Small loans to entrepreneurs in poor communities.
What is the Millennium Development Goals' successor? :: The UN Sustainable Development Goals.
What is foreign direct investment? :: Investment by a company or government into businesses in another country.
What is a free trade zone? :: An area with relaxed tax and tariff rules.
What are the environmental impacts of globalization? :: More emissions from shipping and resource use, and the spread of invasive species.
What is climate refugee? :: A person displaced by climate-related changes.
What is the digital economy? :: Economic activity that relies on digital technology.
What is the gig economy? :: Short-term, flexible work, often through apps.
What is e-commerce? :: Buying and selling over the internet.
What is the role of Amnesty International? :: To defend human rights worldwide.
What is Doctors Without Borders? :: An NGO providing medical help in crisis zones.
What is the International Criminal Court? :: A court that prosecutes genocide, war crimes and crimes against humanity.
What is the Responsibility to Protect (R2P)? :: A UN principle that the international community should act to stop mass atrocities.
What is a trade bloc? :: A group of countries with preferential trade agreements.
What is a nation's sovereignty concern with globalization? :: Loss of control over decisions to international bodies.
What is protectionism? :: Policies that shield domestic industries from foreign competition.
What is a quota? :: A limit on the quantity of imports.
What is the difference between local and global? :: Local relates to a community; global relates to the whole world.
What is an NGO's role in globalization? :: Advocacy, aid, and monitoring of rights and environment.
What is internet censorship? :: Government control over online information.
What is the global commons? :: Shared resources such as oceans, atmosphere and space.
What is the effect of globalization on Indigenous peoples? :: Both opportunities and risks to land, languages and traditions.
What does globalization of agriculture mean? :: Food produced in one place and sold worldwide.
What is the 100-mile diet? :: Eating food grown locally to reduce environmental impact.
What is the impact of the Alberta oil sands on global issues? :: Energy supply and jobs versus emissions and land impact.
What is economic interdependence? :: Countries relying on each other for goods and services.
What is a developing country's debt problem? :: High debt payments that limit spending on health and education.
What is global governance? :: International cooperation to manage global issues.
What is cultural appropriation? :: Adopting elements of another culture without respect or understanding.
What is a perspective? :: A point of view shaped by experiences and values.

## Social Studies 10-2 | Living in a Globalizing World
What is an economy? :: The system of producing, selling and buying goods and services.
What is global trade? :: Buying and selling goods across countries.
What is a Canadian export? :: Oil, lumber, wheat, canola and cars are examples.
What is a Canadian import? :: Cars, electronics and fruit are examples.
What is Canada's largest trading partner? :: The United States.
What is a multinational's effect on local jobs? :: It can create jobs but may also close local businesses.
What is the minimum wage? :: The lowest hourly pay allowed by law.
What is fast fashion? :: Cheaply produced clothes with quick turnaround; often tied to poor labour conditions.
What are the effects of imported cheap goods? :: Lower prices for consumers but competition for local producers.
What is a fair-trade product? :: A product made under fair pay and working conditions.
What is a refugee? :: A person who flees their country due to persecution or war.
What is an immigrant? :: A person who moves permanently to a new country.
Why do people migrate? :: For jobs, safety, family and better opportunities.
What is a brain drain? :: Skilled workers leaving a country for better opportunities.
What is remittance? :: Money sent home by workers abroad.
What is a pandemic's effect on globalization? :: It disrupts supply chains and travel.
What is a supply chain? :: The network of steps from producing to delivering goods.
What is a trade agreement? :: A deal between countries about trade rules.
What is the WTO's role? :: Setting trade rules and settling disputes.
What is humanitarian aid? :: Emergency help such as food and medical supplies.
What is development aid? :: Long-term help to improve economies and services.
What is the Canadian International Development Agency role (now Global Affairs Canada)? :: Delivering Canada's foreign aid.
What is a Canadian peacekeeper? :: A Canadian soldier or official assisting UN peace missions.
What is the Red Cross? :: A humanitarian organization providing relief worldwide.
What is a global issue? :: A problem affecting many countries, like climate change.
What is pollution's cross-border effect? :: Air and water pollution can travel across borders.
What is climate change's effect on Canada's North? :: Melting permafrost and ice, affecting Inuit communities.
What is a Truth and Reconciliation Calls to Action's aim? :: To redress harms of residential schools and improve relationships.
What is an Indigenous worldview? :: A view that emphasizes connection to land, community and stewardship.
What is globalization's effect on language? :: Dominant languages spread while some languages decline.
What is a global brand? :: A brand recognized worldwide, like a famous soft drink.
What is a cultural festival's value? :: It preserves and shares traditions.
What is the role of social media? :: Connecting people globally but also spreading misinformation.
What is a digital citizen? :: A person who uses technology responsibly and ethically.
What is cyberbullying? :: Bullying through digital platforms.
What is the effect of globalization on youth? :: Shared trends and music but pressure to conform to global culture.
What is a world heritage site? :: A place recognized by UNESCO as having outstanding value.
What is UNESCO? :: The UN agency for education, science and culture.
What is UNICEF? :: The UN agency helping children.
What is the WHO? :: The World Health Organization.
What is a human rights violation? :: An action that denies a person's basic rights.
What is the role of the media in global awareness? :: It informs people about distant events.
What is the importance of critical thinking online? :: To check sources and avoid false information.
What is an act of global citizenship? :: Volunteering, fundraising or advocating for global causes.
What is the ecological footprint of Canadians? :: High compared with many countries.
What does consumerism mean? :: A focus on buying goods.
What is a boycott? :: Refusing to buy products to protest a company's actions.
What is a petition? :: A written request signed by many people to authorities.

## Social Studies 20-1 | Perspectives on Nationalism
What was the French Revolution's link to nationalism? :: It promoted the idea that the nation, not the king, holds power.
What is the "nation" in the French Revolution? :: The people sharing rights and identity.
What was the Napoleonic era's effect on nationalism? :: Conquests spread nationalist ideas and stirred resistance.
What was the unification of Germany? :: In 1871, German states joined under Prussia.
What was the unification of Italy? :: By 1870, Italian states were joined into one kingdom.
What was the Scramble for Africa? :: European powers' colonization of Africa in the late 1800s.
What was the Berlin Conference? :: 1884-85 meeting where Europe divided Africa without African input.
What was the Opium Wars' importance? :: British imperialism forced trade on China.
What is the spheres of influence idea? :: Foreign powers controlling trade and politics in parts of another country.
What is jingoism? :: Aggressive, belligerent nationalism.
What was the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand? :: The 1914 event that triggered WWI.
What was the Triple Entente? :: Alliance of France, Britain and Russia.
What was the Triple Alliance? :: Germany, Austria-Hungary and Italy.
What was the Paris Peace Conference? :: The 1919 conference that produced the Treaty of Versailles.
What was the war guilt clause? :: Germany was forced to accept blame for WWI.
What were mandates? :: Territories governed by victors on behalf of the League of Nations.
What is appeasement? :: Giving concessions to an aggressor to avoid war.
What was the Munich Agreement? :: 1938 deal letting Germany take part of Czechoslovakia.
What was the Nuremberg Trials' outcome? :: Nazi leaders were tried for war crimes and crimes against humanity.
What is the Universal Declaration of Human Rights? :: A 1948 UN statement of basic rights.
What is the Marshall Plan? :: US aid to rebuild Western Europe after WWII.
What is NATO? :: A military alliance of North American and European nations.
What was the Suez Crisis' importance for Canada? :: Lester Pearson's peacekeeping proposal helped end it.
What is decolonization? :: The process by which colonies gained independence.
What was India's independence leader known for? :: Mahatma Gandhi's non-violent resistance.
What was apartheid? :: South Africa's system of racial segregation.
Who was Nelson Mandela? :: The anti-apartheid leader who became South Africa's first Black president.
What was the October Crisis of 1970? :: FLQ kidnappings in Quebec, leading to the War Measures Act.
What is the sovereignty-association idea? :: Quebec independence with an economic partnership with Canada.
What was the 1995 Quebec referendum result? :: A narrow "No" vote (about 50.6%).
What is the Clarity Act? :: A 2000 law on conditions for negotiating Quebec secession.
What is the Meech Lake Accord? :: A 1987 constitutional proposal that failed in 1990.
What is western alienation? :: The feeling that the West has less influence in Canada.
What is the Idle No More movement? :: An Indigenous-led movement from 2012 for rights and environmental protection.
What is the White Paper of 1969? :: A federal proposal to end the Indian Act that was rejected by Indigenous leaders.
What is the Red Paper? :: The Alberta Indian Chiefs' 1970 response to the White Paper.
What is the Nunavut creation? :: A territory created in 1999 with an Inuit majority.
What is the Nisga'a Treaty? :: A 2000 modern treaty giving Nisga'a self-government in B.C.
What is the Canadian flag debate of 1964? :: A national debate before adopting the maple leaf flag in 1965.
What is the role of symbols in nationalism? :: They unite a people through shared identity.
What is Acadian nationalism? :: Pride in Acadian heritage in Atlantic Canada.
What is Quebec's distinct society claim? :: A claim that Quebec's unique language and culture need protection.
What is the importance of the Charter for minority rights? :: It protects language and equality rights.
What is nationalism's effect on a country's loyalty? :: It can unify people but exclude minorities.
What is cultural genocide? :: Destroying a group's language and culture.
What is the Truth and Reconciliation Commission's role? :: To document residential school history and guide reconciliation.
What is the Meeting of Treaty chiefs? :: Gatherings where Indigenous leaders discuss treaty rights with governments.

## Social Studies 20-2 | Understandings of Nationalism

What are examples of national symbols? :: Flags, anthems, monuments and emblems.
What is national identity? :: A shared sense of belonging to a nation.
What factors build national identity? :: Language, history, culture, geography and shared values.
What was the Canadian Confederation's role? :: It joined colonies into the Dominion of Canada.
What was the Red River Resistance? :: Louis Riel's Metis defence of rights in 1869-70.
Who was Louis Riel? :: Metis leader who founded Manitoba; executed in 1885.
What was the North-West Resistance? :: The 1885 conflict between Metis and Cree and the Canadian government.
What is the Numbered Treaties' purpose? :: To open Prairie land for settlement and set reserves.
What is the Chinese Head Tax? :: A fee charged to Chinese immigrants to Canada from 1885 to 1923.
What is the Komagata Maru incident? :: A 1914 ship of South Asian migrants denied entry into Canada.
What is the internment of Japanese Canadians? :: In WWII, Japanese Canadians were forced from the West Coast.
What is the Ukrainian Canadian internment? :: Internment of Ukrainians during WWI.
What was the effect of WWI on Canadian identity? :: It increased national pride and independence from Britain.
What was the Statute of Westminster? :: A 1931 law giving Canada legislative independence.
What was conscription? :: Compulsory military service.
What was the conscription crisis of 1917? :: A divide between English and French Canada over compulsory service.
What was Canada's role in WWII? :: A major ally in Europe and the Pacific.
What was D-Day for Canadians? :: Juno Beach, 1944, a key Canadian landing in Normandy.
What is Remembrance Day? :: November 11, honouring those who served.
What is the Canada Day holiday? :: July 1, celebrating Confederation.
What is the Canadian Citizenship Act? :: 1947 law creating Canadian citizenship.
What is the Official Languages Act? :: 1969 law making English and French official federal languages.
What is Bill 101 purpose? :: To make French the official language of Quebec.
What is the Charter's effect on language? :: It protects minority language education rights.
What is the Quiet Revolution? :: 1960s modernization of Quebec society.
What is the Front de liberation du Quebec? :: A militant separatist group in the 1960s-70s.
What is the Canadian Multiculturalism Act? :: A 1988 law protecting cultural diversity.
What is the point system for immigration? :: A system scoring skills, education and language for selecting immigrants.
What is a multicultural society? :: A society where many cultures live together.
What is the Indian Act's effect on identity? :: It controlled status and ways of life.
What is the 1960 right to vote for First Nations? :: Status Indians gained the right to vote federally without losing status.
What is the National Film Board's role? :: Telling Canadian stories to build identity.
What is the CBC's role? :: A public broadcaster promoting Canadian culture.
What is Hockey Night in Canada? :: A long-running broadcast that became a symbol of Canadian culture.
What is the Group of Seven? :: Canadian painters known for landscapes of the early 1900s.
What is a monument? :: A structure commemorating a person or event.
What is Vimy Memorial? :: A memorial in France honouring Canadian soldiers.
What are loyalty and patriotism? :: Strong attachment to one's country.
What is the importance of Treaty 7? :: It was signed in 1877 in southern Alberta with the Blackfoot and others.
What is the Francophone community in Alberta? :: About 80,000 French speakers, with communities like St. Paul and Bonnyville.
What is Alberta's role in Confederation? :: It became a province in 1905.
What is an anthem? :: A song expressing national pride, like O Canada.
What is the role of civic participation? :: Voting, volunteering and joining in community life.

Who was John Locke? :: Philosopher who argued for natural rights to life, liberty and property.
Who was Thomas Hobbes? :: Philosopher who argued for a strong government to avoid chaos.
Who was Jean-Jacques Rousseau? :: Philosopher of the "general will" and popular sovereignty.
Who was John Stuart Mill? :: A liberal philosopher who defended individual liberty and free speech.
What is the Declaration of the Rights of Man? :: A 1789 French document declaring equal rights.
What is the American Declaration of Independence idea? :: People have unalienable rights and governments need their consent.
What is laissez-faire? :: Minimal government interference in the economy.
What is the invisible hand? :: Adam Smith's idea that self-interest in markets benefits society.
What is the welfare state? :: A system in which government provides social supports.
What is the mixed economy? :: A blend of private enterprise and government intervention.
What is class struggle? :: Marx's idea of conflict between owners and workers.
What is the proletariat? :: Marx's term for the working class.
What is the bourgeoisie? :: Marx's term for the owning class.
What is a vanguard party? :: Lenin's idea of a party leading the revolution.
What was the Russian Revolution of 1917? :: The overthrow of the tsar leading to a communist state.
What was Stalin's Soviet economy? :: A command economy with collectivized farms and five-year plans.
What was the Holodomor? :: The Soviet-engineered famine in Ukraine in 1932-33.
What was the Cultural Revolution? :: Mao's 1966-76 campaign in China that caused massive upheaval.
What is fascism's key features? :: Dictatorship, extreme nationalism, militarism and suppression of dissent.
What was Nazi Germany's ideology? :: Fascism combined with racial antisemitism and expansionism.
What was Mussolini's role? :: The leader of Fascist Italy from 1922.
What is authoritarianism? :: A system where a leader or elite holds power without being accountable.
What is a one-party state? :: A system with a single legal political party.
What is neo-conservatism? :: A modern conservatism favouring free markets and strong national defence.
What is neoliberal globalization? :: Policy of open markets, deregulation and privatization.
What was the Reagan-Thatcher era? :: 1980s policy favouring tax cuts, deregulation and privatization.
What is the Third Way? :: A centrist approach combining market ideas with social policies.
What is environmentalism as an ideology? :: A view that sustainability should guide policy.
What is feminism? :: The movement for gender equality.
What is a theocracy? :: A government based on religious law.
What is theocratic ideology? :: Belief that religion should guide the state.
What is a populist movement? :: A movement claiming to represent ordinary people against elites.
What is the CCF party? :: A Canadian socialist party founded in the 1930s that became the NDP.
What is the Social Credit? :: An Alberta movement led by William Aberhart that governed from 1935.
What is the Reform Party? :: A Western-based populist party founded in 1987.
What is the NDP? :: A Canadian social democratic party.
What is the Conservative Party's core idea? :: Fiscal restraint, tradition and limited government.
What is the Liberal Party's core idea? :: Individual rights and a balance of market and social policy.
What is the Bloc Quebecois? :: A federal party advocating for Quebec's interests.
What is the Winnipeg General Strike of 1919? :: A major labour protest for better wages and collective bargaining.
What is collective bargaining? :: Negotiations between unions and employers.
What is the Great Depression's effect on ideology in Canada? :: It grew support for socialism and government intervention.
What is the Keynesian approach? :: Government spending to stimulate the economy in downturns.
What is the Cold War's proxy war? :: A conflict where superpowers back opposing sides, like Korea and Vietnam.
What is the fall of the Berlin Wall? :: 1989 event symbolizing the end of the Cold War.
What is propaganda's role in ideology? :: To shape public opinion.
What is political socialization? :: How people form their political beliefs.
What is the difference between left and right wing? :: Left stresses equality and government role; right stresses liberty and markets.
