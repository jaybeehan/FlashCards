## Social Studies 9 | Issues for Canadians: Governance and Rights
What is democracy? :: A system of government where citizens hold power, usually by voting for representatives.
What is the Canadian system of government called? :: A parliamentary democracy and constitutional monarchy.
What are the three levels of government in Canada? :: Federal, provincial/territorial and municipal (plus Indigenous governments).
What are the three branches of government? :: Legislative (makes laws), executive (carries out laws), judicial (interprets laws).
Who is the head of state in Canada? :: The monarch, represented by the Governor General.
Who is the head of government? :: The Prime Minister.
What is the House of Commons? :: The elected lower house of Parliament where MPs debate and vote on bills.
What is the Senate? :: The appointed upper house that reviews bills ("sober second thought").
What is a bill? :: A proposed law.
What is the Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms? :: Part of the 1982 Constitution that protects fundamental rights and freedoms.
Name some fundamental freedoms in the Charter. :: Freedom of religion, expression, peaceful assembly, association, press.
What are democratic rights? :: The right to vote and to run for office.
What is the notwithstanding clause? :: Section 33 lets governments override some Charter rights for five years.
What is a majority government? :: A governing party holding more than half of the seats.
What is a minority government? :: The governing party has the most seats but not a majority.
What is a federal riding? :: A voting district represented by one MP.
What is the Indian Act? :: 1876 federal law that controls many aspects of First Nations life; criticized for oppression.
What are treaty rights? :: Rights of Indigenous peoples set out in treaties with the Crown.
What are Treaties 6, 7 and 8? :: Treaties covering most of Alberta.
What is Aboriginal self-government? :: Indigenous peoples governing their own affairs.
What is the Truth and Reconciliation Commission? :: A commission (2008-2015) examining residential schools; it issued 94 Calls to Action.
What are collective rights? :: Rights held by groups, such as French language rights.
What are Official Languages? :: English and French in federal institutions (Official Languages Act).
What is the Supreme Court's role? :: It is Canada's highest court, interpreting law and the Charter.

## Social Studies 9 | Issues for Canadians: Economic Systems
What is an economic system? :: How a society decides what to produce, how, and for whom.
What are the three basic economic questions? :: What to produce? How to produce? For whom?
What is a traditional economy? :: Production based on custom and subsistence.
What is a command economy? :: The government makes economic decisions.
What is a market economy? :: Individuals and businesses decide through supply and demand.
What is a mixed economy? :: Combines private markets with government involvement; Canada's system.
What is scarcity? :: Limited resources versus unlimited wants.
What is opportunity cost? :: The value of the next best alternative given up.
What is supply? :: The amount of a product producers will offer at various prices.
What is demand? :: The amount consumers will buy at various prices.
What happens to price when demand rises and supply is fixed? :: Price rises.
What is inflation? :: A general rise in prices over time.
What is GDP? :: Gross Domestic Product: the total value of goods and services produced in a country.
What is a recession? :: A significant economic decline lasting months.
What is a tax? :: Money collected by government to pay for services.
What are Crown corporations? :: Government-owned businesses, like Canada Post.
What is free trade? :: Trade with few or no tariffs or barriers.
What is CUSMA? :: The Canada-United States-Mexico Agreement (replaced NAFTA).
What are natural resources? :: Materials from nature such as oil, forests, minerals.
What is the staples economy? :: An economy that depends on exporting raw resources.
What is entrepreneurship? :: Starting and running a business.
What is a monopoly? :: A market with only one seller.
What does the Bank of Canada do? :: Sets monetary policy and the key interest rate.
What is a labour union? :: A group of workers who bargain together for wages and conditions.

## Social Studies 10-1 | Perspectives on Globalization
What is globalization? :: The growing interconnection of the world's economies, cultures and politics.
What are the main drivers of globalization? :: Technology, trade, transportation, communication and investment.
What is a multinational corporation? :: A company operating in several countries.
What is the WTO? :: The World Trade Organization, which governs rules of global trade.
What is the UN? :: The United Nations, an organization promoting peace and cooperation among countries.
What is a free-trade agreement? :: A deal reducing tariffs and trade barriers between countries.
What is cultural diffusion? :: The spread of cultural traits between societies.
What is cultural homogenization? :: Cultures becoming more alike under global influences.
What is a tariff? :: A tax on imports.
What is outsourcing? :: Hiring other countries or firms to do work to cut costs.
What is a sweatshop? :: A factory with poor pay and unsafe working conditions.
What is fair trade? :: Trading that ensures fair prices and conditions for producers.
What is sustainability? :: Meeting present needs without harming future generations.
What is the digital divide? :: The gap between people with and without internet access.
What are human rights? :: Rights belonging to all people, set out in the Universal Declaration of Human Rights.
What is the Universal Declaration of Human Rights? :: A 1948 UN document listing basic rights for everyone.
What is a refugee? :: A person forced to flee their country for fear of persecution.
What is the Kyoto Protocol / Paris Agreement? :: International agreements to limit greenhouse gas emissions.
What does "think globally, act locally" mean? :: Consider global issues but take action in your community.
What is a nation-state? :: A state whose people share a common identity.
What is a pandemic? :: A disease outbreak spreading across countries.
What is Indigenous perspective on globalization? :: Concern about impacts on land, language and traditional ways of life.
What is neo-colonialism? :: Powerful countries or firms exerting economic influence over weaker ones.
What is a worldview? :: A way of seeing and interpreting the world.

## Social Studies 10-2 | Living in a Globalizing World
What does globalization mean? :: Increasing worldwide links in trade, culture, technology and politics.
What is a global citizen? :: Someone who acts with awareness of global issues and responsibilities.
What is a multinational company? :: A business with operations in many countries.
What is trade? :: Exchange of goods and services.
What is an import? :: A good brought into a country.
What is an export? :: A good sent out to be sold in another country.
What is a tariff? :: A tax on imported goods.
What is foreign aid? :: Help given from one country to another.
What is cultural diversity? :: Variety of cultures in a society.
What is cultural loss? :: When languages and traditions fade, for example because of dominant cultures.
What is multiculturalism? :: Canadian policy supporting cultural diversity.
What is the UN's goal? :: To maintain peace, protect rights and encourage cooperation.
What is a non-governmental organization (NGO)? :: A non-profit group working on issues such as Red Cross or Amnesty.
How has technology affected globalization? :: It sped communication, trade and cultural sharing.
What are the pros of globalization? :: Cheaper goods, jobs, cultural exchange, shared innovation.
What are the cons of globalization? :: Job loss, exploitation, environmental damage, loss of culture.
What does "ethical consumer" mean? :: A shopper who considers labour and environmental impacts.
What is poverty? :: Lacking resources needed for a basic standard of living.
What is a developing country? :: A country with lower income and less industrialization.
What are the UN Sustainable Development Goals? :: 17 global goals for ending poverty, protecting the planet and ensuring prosperity by 2030.
What is climate change? :: Long-term warming caused largely by human greenhouse gas emissions.
What is human trafficking? :: The illegal trade and exploitation of people.
What is child labour? :: Work that deprives children of schooling and health.
What can individuals do to be responsible global citizens? :: Learn, vote, volunteer, buy ethically, and speak out.

## Social Studies 20-1 | Perspectives on Nationalism
What is nationalism? :: Strong pride and loyalty toward one's nation or people.
What is a nation? :: A group sharing identity such as language, culture, history or territory.
What is a nation-state? :: A state whose population largely shares one national identity.
What is self-determination? :: A people's right to govern themselves.
What is imperialism? :: Extending a country's power by conquering or controlling other lands.
What is ultranationalism? :: Extreme, aggressive nationalism that harms others.
What is internationalism? :: Cooperation among nations.
What caused WWI (MAIN)? :: Militarism, Alliances, Imperialism, Nationalism.
What was the Treaty of Versailles? :: The 1919 treaty ending WWI; it punished Germany.
What was the League of Nations? :: A post-WWI organization meant to keep peace; it failed to stop WWII.
What was the Holocaust? :: The Nazi genocide of six million Jews and others.
What is genocide? :: The deliberate destruction of a national, ethnic or religious group.
What was the Rwandan genocide? :: 1994 killing of about 800,000 Tutsi.
What were the Quiet Revolution and Quebec nationalism? :: 1960s changes in Quebec toward stronger French identity and secularism.
What was the Charlottetown Accord? :: A 1992 proposal to amend the constitution that was rejected in a referendum.
What is the Indian Act's effect on Indigenous nationalism? :: It suppressed Indigenous governance and culture, fuelling resistance.
What is Francophone nationalism? :: Efforts to protect French language and culture in Canada.
What is Quebec's Bill 101? :: A 1977 law making French the official language in Quebec.
What is a referendum? :: A direct vote on a single question.
What is Pan-Africanism? :: Movement for unity among African peoples.
What is the Vimy Ridge importance? :: A 1917 victory often seen as a key moment in Canadian nationhood.
What are the Calls to Action? :: 94 recommendations for reconciliation from the Truth and Reconciliation Commission.
What is propaganda? :: Information spread to promote a cause or view.
What is the Oka crisis? :: A 1990 land dispute involving Mohawk people and the Quebec and Canadian governments.

## Social Studies 20-2 | Understandings of Nationalism
What is nationalism? :: Pride in and loyalty to one's nation.
What is identity? :: How people see themselves and are seen by others.
What is a nation? :: A group sharing culture, language and history.
What is a nation-state? :: A country whose people mostly share one identity.
What is imperialism? :: One country controlling others for power and resources.
What is a colony? :: A territory controlled by a foreign country.
What is self-determination? :: A people's right to decide their own government.
What was WWI? :: A global war from 1914 to 1918.
What is internationalism? :: Cooperation among countries.
What is the United Nations? :: An organization founded in 1945 to maintain peace and cooperation.
What is a peacekeeper? :: A soldier or official helping maintain peace under the UN.
What did Lester B. Pearson do? :: He proposed the first UN peacekeeping force and won a Nobel Peace Prize.
What is the Francophone identity in Canada? :: Pride in the French language, culture and history, mainly in Quebec.
What is a sovereign state? :: A state with full control over its territory and affairs.
What is a treaty? :: A formal agreement between nations.
What was the Residential School system? :: Government-funded Church-run schools that tried to assimilate Indigenous children.
What is assimilation? :: Forcing a group to adopt the dominant culture.
What is reconciliation? :: Restoring respectful relationships, especially with Indigenous peoples.
What is a nationalist symbol? :: A flag, anthem or emblem representing a nation.
What is cultural preservation? :: Protecting a group's language and traditions.
What is the Canadian flag's year? :: 1965 (the maple leaf flag).
How did the 1982 Constitution change Canada? :: It patriated the constitution and added the Charter.
What is a separatist movement? :: A group seeking independence from a country.
What does Remembrance Day honour? :: Canadians who served and died in war.

## Social Studies 30-1 | Perspectives on Ideology
What is ideology? :: A set of beliefs about how society and government should work.
What is liberalism? :: An ideology emphasizing individual rights and freedoms.
What is classical liberalism? :: Emphasis on limited government, free markets and individual liberty.
What is modern liberalism? :: Liberalism accepting government action to reduce inequality.
What is collectivism? :: Putting the group or community ahead of the individual.
What is communism? :: Ideology seeking a classless society with collective ownership.
What is fascism? :: An authoritarian ultra-nationalist ideology with dictatorial power.
What is socialism? :: Ideology favouring collective or public ownership and greater equality.
What is capitalism? :: An economic system based on private ownership and markets.
What is a command economy? :: An economy planned and controlled by the state.
What is conservatism? :: An ideology valuing tradition and gradual change.
What is neo-liberalism? :: Modern revival of free-market ideas: deregulation, privatization.
Who was Adam Smith? :: Author of The Wealth of Nations; promoted the free market ("invisible hand").
Who was Karl Marx? :: Philosopher who wrote The Communist Manifesto and criticized capitalism.
What was the Industrial Revolution's effect on ideology? :: It created new classes and sparked ideas such as socialism.
What was the Great Depression? :: Global economic crisis of the 1930s.
What is Keynesian economics? :: Government spending to stabilize the economy.
What is a totalitarian state? :: A government with absolute control over public and private life.
What is the Cold War? :: The tense rivalry between the USA and USSR (1947-1991).
What are individual rights? :: Rights belonging to each person, such as free speech.
What are collective rights? :: Rights that belong to groups.
What is Alberta's relation to ideology? :: Alberta has a strong tradition of conservatism and populist movements such as Social Credit.
What is the rule of law? :: Everyone, including leaders, is subject to law.
What is political spectrum? :: A scale from left (more equality, government role) to right (more individual liberty, less government).

## Social Studies 30-2 | Understandings of Ideology
What is an ideology? :: A system of beliefs about government and society.
What is liberalism? :: Belief in individual rights and freedoms.
What is communism? :: Belief in collective ownership and a classless society.
What is fascism? :: A dictatorial ultra-nationalist ideology.
What is capitalism? :: An economic system with private ownership and markets.
What is socialism? :: Favouring public ownership and equality.
What is democracy? :: Government by the people, usually through elections.
What is a dictatorship? :: A government under one ruler with absolute power.
What was World War II's cause? :: Aggression by fascist states and failure of appeasement.
What was the Cold War? :: A long conflict between capitalist USA and communist USSR without direct war.
What was the Great Depression? :: A 1930s global economic collapse.
What is the political left/right? :: Left: more government equality focus. Right: more individual freedom, markets.
What is a welfare state? :: A government that supports citizens through social programs.
What is propaganda? :: Messages used to shape opinion.
What is civil disobedience? :: Peaceful refusal to follow a law to protest it.
What are human rights? :: Basic rights belonging to every person.
What is the Charter of Rights and Freedoms? :: Canada's 1982 bill of rights.
What is the role of citizens in democracy? :: Voting, discussing issues, holding leaders accountable.
What is a political party? :: An organized group seeking power to influence government.
What is the rule of law? :: Everyone must obey laws, even leaders.
What is individualism? :: Valuing individual freedom and independence.
What is collectivism? :: Valuing group needs and goals.
What is the role of the free press? :: Informing citizens and holding power accountable.
What is the Social Credit Party? :: An Alberta party that governed 1935-1971.
