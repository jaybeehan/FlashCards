## Chemistry 30 | Thermochemical Changes
What is thermochemistry? :: The study of heat changes in chemical reactions.
What is enthalpy? :: The heat content of a system at constant pressure.
What is delta H? :: The enthalpy change of a reaction.
What is the sign of delta H for exothermic reactions? :: Negative.
What is the sign of delta H for endothermic reactions? :: Positive.
What is the formula for heat transfer? :: Q = m c delta T
What is specific heat capacity? :: Energy needed to raise 1 g of a substance by 1 degree C.
What is the specific heat capacity of water? :: 4.19 J/(g C)
Find Q: 50 g water heated by 20 degrees C. :: 4190 J
Find delta T: 837 J into 100 g of water. :: 2.0 degrees C
Find the mass: 2095 J raises water by 5 degrees C. :: 100 g
What is a calorimeter? :: A device that measures heat transfer.
What is calorimetry? :: Measuring heat released or absorbed.
What is a coffee-cup calorimeter? :: A simple calorimeter at constant pressure.
What is a bomb calorimeter? :: A calorimeter measuring combustion at constant volume.
How do you find molar enthalpy? :: delta H = Q / n
Find molar enthalpy: 5000 J released by 0.10 mol. :: -50 kJ/mol
What is a thermochemical equation? :: A balanced equation including the enthalpy change.
How do you write delta H in an equation? :: As a term on the product side (exothermic) or reactant side (endothermic).
What happens to delta H when an equation is reversed? :: Its sign changes.
What happens to delta H when an equation is doubled? :: It is doubled.
What is Hess's law? :: Total enthalpy change is the sum of the steps.
How do you use Hess's law? :: Add equations and their delta H values to get the target.
What is the standard enthalpy of formation? :: Enthalpy change when one mole forms from its elements in standard states.
What is delta H of formation for an element? :: Zero.
What is the formula using formation enthalpies? :: delta H = sum(n delta Hf products) - sum(n delta Hf reactants)
What is enthalpy of combustion? :: Heat released when one mole burns completely.
What is enthalpy of vaporization? :: Energy to vaporize one mole of liquid.
What is enthalpy of fusion? :: Energy to melt one mole of solid.
What is the heat equation for a phase change? :: Q = n delta H (or m x specific latent heat).
What is bond energy? :: Energy needed to break one mole of a bond.
What is the relationship between bond breaking and energy? :: Breaking bonds absorbs energy; forming bonds releases energy.
How can bond energies estimate delta H? :: Energy of bonds broken minus energy of bonds formed.
What is activation energy? :: The energy barrier needed to start a reaction.
What is an activated complex? :: The unstable arrangement at the peak of the energy barrier.
What does a potential energy diagram show? :: Energy changes along a reaction path.
In an exothermic diagram, which is lower in energy? :: The products.
In an endothermic diagram, which is lower in energy? :: The reactants.
How is delta H shown on the diagram? :: The difference between product and reactant energy.
What does a catalyst do to activation energy? :: Lowers it.
Does a catalyst change delta H? :: No.
What is the first law of thermodynamics? :: Energy is conserved.
What is a system? :: The part of the universe being studied.
What are surroundings? :: Everything outside the system.
What is an open system? :: A system exchanging both matter and energy.
What is a closed system? :: A system exchanging energy but not matter.
What is an isolated system? :: A system exchanging neither matter nor energy.
What is a state function? :: A property depending only on the current state.
What is temperature? :: A measure of average kinetic energy.
What is heat? :: Energy transferred because of a temperature difference.
What is the unit of heat? :: Joule.
Convert 4.18 J to calories. :: About 1 calorie.
What is a food Calorie? :: 1 kilocalorie or about 4184 J.
What is an exothermic example in daily life? :: Burning wood or a hand warmer.
What is an endothermic example in daily life? :: An instant cold pack.
What is a fuel's energy density? :: Energy released per unit mass or volume.
Which releases more energy per gram: hydrogen or gasoline? :: Hydrogen.
What is entropy? :: A measure of disorder.
Which has higher entropy: gas or solid? :: Gas.
What drives spontaneous reactions? :: Release of energy and increased disorder.
What is Gibbs free energy idea? :: Combines enthalpy and entropy to predict spontaneity.
What is a combustion reaction's delta H sign? :: Negative.
Write the combustion of methane. :: CH4 + 2 O2 -> CO2 + 2 H2O + energy
What is the main product of burning hydrogen? :: Water.
What is the heat of neutralization? :: Heat released when acid and base form one mole of water.
What is a typical neutralization delta H? :: About -56 kJ per mole of water.
How do you design a calorimetry experiment? :: Measure mass, specific heat and temperature change.
Why is some heat lost in a real calorimeter? :: Heat escapes to the surroundings and the container.
What is a heat capacity of a calorimeter? :: Energy needed to raise the calorimeter's temperature by 1 degree C.

## Chemistry 30 | Chemical Equilibrium Focusing on Acid-Base Systems
What is chemical equilibrium? :: Forward and reverse reaction rates are equal.
Is a reaction at equilibrium static? :: No; it is dynamic.
What is a reversible reaction? :: A reaction that can go in both directions.
What is the equilibrium law expression? :: K = [products]^coefficients / [reactants]^coefficients
What is Kc? :: The equilibrium constant using concentrations.
Write K for N2 + 3 H2 <-> 2 NH3. :: K = [NH3]^2 / ([N2][H2]^3)
Write K for 2 SO2 + O2 <-> 2 SO3. :: K = [SO3]^2 / ([SO2]^2 [O2])
Are pure solids and liquids included in K? :: No.
What does K much greater than 1 mean? :: Products are favoured.
What does K much less than 1 mean? :: Reactants are favoured.
What is an ICE table? :: A table of initial, change and equilibrium amounts.
What is the reaction quotient Q? :: The ratio calculated like K at any time.
If Q < K, which way does the reaction shift? :: Forward (toward products).
If Q > K, which way does the reaction shift? :: Reverse (toward reactants).
If Q = K, what is the system's state? :: At equilibrium.
State Le Chatelier's principle. :: A system at equilibrium shifts to counter an imposed change.
What happens if you add reactant? :: The equilibrium shifts toward products.
What happens if you remove product? :: The equilibrium shifts toward products.
What happens if you increase pressure? :: It shifts toward the side with fewer gas moles.
What happens if you increase temperature (endothermic forward)? :: It shifts toward products.
What happens if you increase temperature (exothermic forward)? :: It shifts toward reactants.
Does a catalyst change the equilibrium position? :: No; it only speeds reaching equilibrium.
Does adding an inert gas at constant volume shift the equilibrium? :: No.
What does temperature change do to K? :: It changes the value of K.
Do concentration changes change K? :: No.
What is Ksp? :: The solubility product constant.
Write Ksp for AgCl. :: Ksp = [Ag+][Cl-]
Write Ksp for CaF2. :: Ksp = [Ca2+][F-]^2
What is the common ion effect? :: Adding a common ion lowers solubility.
What is a precipitate forming condition? :: When the ion product Q exceeds Ksp.
What is the Bronsted-Lowry acid? :: A proton donor.
What is the Bronsted-Lowry base? :: A proton acceptor.
What is a conjugate acid-base pair? :: Two species differing by one proton.
Give the conjugate base of H2SO4. :: HSO4-
Give the conjugate acid of OH-. :: H2O
What is an amphiprotic species? :: One that can donate or accept a proton.
Give an amphiprotic species. :: Water or HCO3-.
What is Kw? :: [H3O+][OH-] = 1.0 x 10^-14 at 25 degrees C.
What is pH? :: -log[H3O+]
What is pOH? :: -log[OH-]
What is pH + pOH? :: 14
What is the pH of 0.010 mol/L HCl? :: 2.00
What is the pH of 0.010 mol/L NaOH? :: 12.00
What is the pH of a solution with [H3O+] = 3.2 x 10^-5? :: 4.49
What is [H3O+] at pH 8.50? :: 3.2 x 10^-9 mol/L
What is Ka? :: The acid dissociation constant.
Write Ka for HA. :: Ka = [H3O+][A-]/[HA]
What does a large Ka mean? :: A stronger acid.
What is Kb? :: The base dissociation constant.
How are Ka and Kb of a conjugate pair related? :: Ka x Kb = Kw
What is the pH of a weak acid vs a strong acid at equal concentration? :: Higher for the weak acid.
What is percent ionization? :: ([H3O+] at equilibrium / initial [HA]) x 100%.
What is a buffer? :: A solution that resists pH change.
What makes a buffer? :: A weak acid and its conjugate base.
Give a buffer example. :: Acetic acid and sodium acetate.
What is the Henderson-Hasselbalch equation? :: pH = pKa + log([A-]/[HA])
What is the pH when [A-] = [HA]? :: pH = pKa
What is a titration curve? :: A graph of pH versus volume of titrant added.
What is the equivalence point of a strong acid-strong base titration? :: pH 7.
What is the equivalence point pH for a weak acid with strong base? :: Above 7.
What is the equivalence point pH for a weak base with strong acid? :: Below 7.
What is the half-equivalence point? :: Where pH equals pKa for a weak acid.
Which indicator suits a weak acid-strong base titration? :: Phenolphthalein.
What is an indicator's range? :: The pH interval over which it changes colour.
What is a polyprotic acid? :: An acid with more than one ionizable proton.
Give a polyprotic acid. :: H2SO4 or H3PO4.
What is hydrolysis of a salt? :: Reaction of salt ions with water changing pH.
Is NaCl solution acidic, basic or neutral? :: Neutral.
Is NH4Cl solution acidic, basic or neutral? :: Acidic.
Is NaCH3COO solution acidic, basic or neutral? :: Basic.
What is the carbonate buffer system in blood? :: H2CO3 and HCO3- keep blood near pH 7.4.
What is the Haber process? :: Making ammonia from N2 and H2 using equilibrium conditions.
What conditions favour NH3 in the Haber process? :: High pressure and moderate temperature with a catalyst.
